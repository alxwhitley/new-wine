# Gifts of the Spirit — Overview
*A Theological Position Paper for Rhemata*

---

## What It Is

The gifts of the Spirit are supernatural enablements the Holy Spirit gives to believers — not natural talent dressed up in spiritual language, but genuine manifestations of God's own power and knowledge working through an ordinary person. Paul names nine of them directly: the word of wisdom, the word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healing, the working of miracles, prophecy, the discerning of spirits, various kinds of tongues, and the interpretation of tongues (1 Corinthians 12:8-10). Each one is described as a "manifestation of the Spirit" — a way the invisible Spirit of God makes Himself visible through the senses, in a specific moment, for a specific need.

These nine are not the whole list. Scripture describes other, quieter gifts too — serving, teaching, exhorting, giving, leading, showing mercy (Romans 12:6-8), helping and administrating (1 Corinthians 12:28). Some gifts are dramatic and immediately obvious; others look like faithfulness in an ordinary task. Both are the Spirit's own work, and neither is more spiritual than the other.

---

## Given for the Common Good, Not for the Individual

Scripture is direct about why these gifts exist: "to each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good" (1 Corinthians 12:7). Not for personal status. Not for private benefit. The gifts flow through a believer toward everyone else. A gift that only ever serves the person carrying it has drifted from its purpose.

This is also why the gifts cannot become a hierarchy of importance. Paul spends most of a chapter making exactly this point: the body has many parts, and the parts that seem weaker or less presentable are treated with no less honor (1 Corinthians 12:14-25). No gift makes one believer more essential than another.

---

## Sovereignly Given, and Genuinely to Be Desired

Two things are true at once here, and Scripture holds them together rather than choosing one. The Spirit "apportions to each one individually as he wills" (1 Corinthians 12:11) — no believer manufactures a gift by wanting it hard enough, and no gift is owed to anyone on demand. And yet, in the very next breath, believers are told: "earnestly desire the higher gifts" (1 Corinthians 12:31), and specifically to "earnestly desire to prophesy" (1 Corinthians 14:39). Sovereignty does not cancel invitation. God gives as He wills, and He has told His people to ask, seek, and desire what He gives.

---

## Laying On of Hands and Impartation

The laying on of hands is one of the ways gifts are recognized, released, and passed on — not a New Testament novelty, but named among the foundational doctrines of the faith itself (Hebrews 6:1-2). Its purposes in Scripture are varied: commissioning someone for a task (Acts 13:3), receiving the Holy Spirit (Acts 8:17, Acts 19:6), releasing healing (Mark 16:18), and imparting a specific gift or ministry, as when Timothy received his through prophecy together with the laying on of hands (1 Timothy 4:14, 2 Timothy 1:6).

This is a genuinely spiritual act, not a symbolic gesture — and precisely because it carries real weight, Scripture treats it with real caution. Paul's warning to Timothy is blunt: "do not be hasty in the laying on of hands" (1 Timothy 5:22). A practice that can release something real can also be misused carelessly — endorsing someone prematurely, or treating impartation as automatic and mechanical rather than a genuine act of the Spirit working through people who are themselves walking in humility and discernment. The remedy is not to avoid the practice; it is to handle it with the seriousness Scripture itself gives it.

---

## Every Believer Has a Gift; No Believer Has Every Gift

"Are all apostles? Are all prophets?... Do all work miracles? Do all possess gifts of healing? Do all speak with tongues?" Paul's rhetorical questions in 1 Corinthians 12:29-30 expect the answer no. No single believer carries every gift — that was never the design. But this is not a ceiling on ordinary believers so much as a description of how the body actually works: "to each is given" (1 Corinthians 12:7) means every believer carries something, even if no one carries everything. The gifts were distributed across the whole body precisely so the body would need every part of itself.

---

## Common Objections

**"Isn't pursuing spiritual gifts a little showy or spiritually immature?"**
Paul commands the church to pursue them, in the same passage where he insists love is what actually gives them meaning (1 Corinthians 13). Desiring the gifts and pursuing love are not opposites — Scripture places them side by side on purpose. A gift exercised without love becomes noise (1 Corinthians 13:1); that is a warning about how the gifts are used, not a reason to stop wanting them.

**"If the Spirit lives in every believer equally, why do only some people seem to operate in certain gifts?"**
Because the gifts are apportioned "as he wills" (1 Corinthians 12:11), not distributed identically to everyone. The Spirit's presence in every believer is equal; the specific gifts He chooses to manifest through each person are not. That difference is what makes a body a body rather than a room full of identical parts.

**"Isn't 'impartation' just hype — laying on of hands as a spiritual performance?"**
It can be misused that way, and Scripture's own caution against being hasty in the practice exists precisely because that risk is real. But the answer to a genuine thing being counterfeited or performed carelessly is discernment, not abandoning something Scripture itself names as a foundational, ongoing practice of the church.

---

## What This Is Not Saying

This is not a claim that some gifts rank above others in importance, or that a believer without a dramatic gift is less spiritual than one who has one. The body needs every part, including the parts that never draw attention.

This is not a claim that a gift can be produced on command through the right technique, formula, or hands laid at the right moment. The Spirit gives as He wills; no practice, however biblical, obligates Him.

This is not permission to treat every claimed gift, or every act of laying on of hands, as automatically genuine. Real things can be counterfeited or handled carelessly — that calls for discernment, not blanket suspicion or blanket credulity.

And this is not a claim that operating in a gift is the measure of spiritual maturity. Scripture is explicit that a gift exercised without love profits nothing. Love is the context that gives every gift its meaning — not a gift's intensity or visibility.

---

## Living in Light of It

The point of these gifts was never to make a handful of believers seem impressive. It was to give the whole body of Christ real, working access to God's own power and wisdom, distributed across every member so that no one carries it alone and no one is left out of it. Desire them. Use them for others. Let love be what holds the whole thing together.
