# Prophecy and the Prophetic
*A Theological Position Paper for Rhemata*

---

## What It Is

Prophecy is God speaking to His people in a way that goes beyond what the natural mind could know on its own — through a word, an impression, a picture, a sense of God's heart for a moment or a person — spoken out for the strengthening of the church. Words of knowledge and words of wisdom are closely related expressions of the same Spirit: a word of knowledge is supernatural insight into a fact otherwise unknown; a word of wisdom is supernatural insight into what to do with it. All three flow from the same source — the Spirit revealing what the natural mind has no way of knowing.

Scripture is not shy about wanting this. Moses himself said it first: "Would that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD would put his Spirit on them!" (Numbers 11:29). Centuries later, that longing became a promise. Joel foresaw a day when the Spirit would be poured out on all flesh, and "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (Joel 2:28). Peter stood up at Pentecost and declared that day had arrived (Acts 2:17-18). Prophecy was never meant to be rare.

---

## Its Purpose Is to Build the Church Up

Paul is direct about why this gift exists: "the one who prophesies builds up the church" (1 Corinthians 14:4), and "the one who prophesies speaks to people for their upbuilding and encouragement and consolation" (1 Corinthians 14:3). Prophecy is not primarily about spectacle or prediction. Its normal, everyday purpose is to strengthen, comfort, and encourage — to say to someone what God is actually thinking about them, at a moment when they most need to hear it.

Because of this, Paul's instruction to the church is not caution but appetite: "earnestly desire the higher gifts" (1 Corinthians 12:31), and specifically, "earnestly desire to prophesy" (1 Corinthians 14:39). Of all the gifts Paul discusses, this is the one he tells the whole church to actively want.

---

## Available to the Whole Church, Not a Closed Office

Scripture holds two things together that are easy to flatten into a contradiction: not everyone holds the office of a prophet, but every believer can prophesy. "Are all prophets?" Paul asks rhetorically in 1 Corinthians 12:29 — the expected answer is no. But two chapters later, addressing the whole congregation, he says: "you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all be encouraged" (1 Corinthians 14:31). These are not competing claims. The office of a prophet is a distinct, weightier calling within the church (Ephesians 4:11) — but the simple activity of prophesying, in the sense of speaking an edifying word given by the Spirit in the moment, is available to any believer led by the Spirit to do so.

---

## It Comes Through People, and People Are Imperfect

Prophecy is not dictation. God speaks through a real person's mind, personality, and vocabulary — the way He worked through the human authors of Scripture itself, without erasing who they were in the process. That means a prophetic word today, unlike the finished, closed canon of Scripture, is not expected to be perfectly pure in every detail. It reflects the mix of divine origin and human vessel.

This is exactly why Scripture never hands prophecy a blank check of authority. "Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:20-21). "Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said" (1 Corinthians 14:29). Prophecy is welcomed, not policed out of fear — but it is also never exempted from being tested against Scripture, weighed by mature believers, and held loosely until confirmed.

## An unresolved tension worth naming honestly

One thread develops a fairly structured framework in which prophecy can appropriately give specific personal direction, provided it passes real safeguards: confirmed by other mature prophetic voices, tested against Scripture, weighed rather than obeyed on the spot. Another thread, from within the same broader circle of teaching, leans the opposite direction on emphasis — favoring direct, personal dependence on the Lord's own leading over relying on any human intermediary, including a prophetic word, for the biggest decisions of a person's life, and explicitly cautioning against the kind of reliance on someone else's voice that can shade into pride or control. Both instincts are present, and both are trying to protect something real — freedom to hear God through others, and freedom from being steered by others. This paper does not resolve how far "directive" prophecy should be allowed to go before this gets settled deliberately.

## Common Objections

**"Doesn't this open the door to people manipulating others with 'God told me to tell you'?"**
That risk is real, which is exactly why testing prophecy is not optional in this teaching — it is commanded. A word that cannot be tested, that demands blind obedience, or that pressures someone toward a decision rather than confirming what God is already doing in them, is being handled wrongly regardless of whether it happened to be accurate. Authority belongs to Scripture and to the discernment of the gathered church, never to the person speaking the word.

**"If prophecy is fallible, why bother with it at all — why not just rely on the Bible?"**
Because Scripture itself tells the church to pursue this gift, not avoid it (1 Corinthians 14:1, 39). Fallibility is a reason to test a word, not a reason to silence it. The Bible is the fixed, final standard every prophetic word is measured against — but Scripture never claims it replaces God's ongoing, personal speech to His people; it says the opposite, and gives instructions for how to receive that speech responsibly.

**"Isn't this just spiritual guesswork dressed up as revelation?"**
Not when it functions the way Scripture describes. Genuine prophecy is specific enough, and consistent enough with what God is actually doing, to be recognizably from Him rather than a vague, safe generality anyone could have guessed. That said, this teaching does not claim every word spoken in a meeting under this label is genuine — which is precisely the point of testing it rather than assuming it.

---

## What This Is Not Saying

This is not a claim that a prophetic word carries the same authority as Scripture, or that it can override Scripture, sound teaching, or a person's own responsibility to seek God directly. Scripture judges every prophecy — prophecy never judges Scripture.

This is not permission to treat every impression or hunch as "a word from the Lord" without any testing. Confidence in the reality of the gift is not the same as certainty about a specific instance of it.

This is not a claim that prophecy is reserved for a spiritual elite. The office of a prophet is a distinct calling, but the simple activity of speaking an edifying word from the Spirit is available to any believer walking in step with Him.

This is not a substitute for a person's own relationship with God, their own reading of Scripture, or their own responsibility before Him for the decisions they make. A prophetic word, even a genuine one, informs — it does not replace a person's own walk with the Lord.

---

## Living in Light of It

The heart behind this gift was never spectacle. It was always this: God still speaks, He speaks to build His people up, and He is not content to leave that speech locked in a book from centuries ago when He can say something specific, timely, and personal to someone who needs to hear it today. Pursue it. Test it. Let it do what it was given to do — strengthen, encourage, and comfort the church God is building.
