## Section-by-section sourcing

- **What It Is / the nine gifts + the broader charismata list** — Prince,
  almost entirely ("Explanation Of Greek Word Charisma," "Twenty-Six New
  Testament Charismata" for the Romans 12 list).
- **Given for the common good** — scripture-anchored (1 Corinthians 12:7,
  14-25), broadly consistent with everyone who touches the topic; genuine
  low-grade consensus, not one teacher's idiosyncratic point.
- **Sovereignly given, yet to be desired** — this specific "both/and"
  framing (12:11 "as he wills" held together with 12:31/14:39 "earnestly
  desire") is my own synthesis of two passages Prince also cites
  separately; he doesn't put them side-by-side in quite this way himself,
  so this section's structure is more mine than his, even though every
  individual claim in it is scripture, not invention.
- **Laying on of hands and impartation** — almost entirely Prince
  ("Transmitting God's Power," "The Six Foundation Stones," "Analysis of
  Hebrews: Chapter 6"). Notably, Prince himself supplies BOTH the strong
  affirmative case (real historical precedent, real purposes) AND the
  caution (his own material warns of "spiritual contamination" and
  "endorsing someone unworthy") — this balance in the draft reflects one
  teacher holding both sides of the tension himself, not two teachers in
  disagreement. Worth knowing this whole section rests on one voice, even
  though it doesn't read as one-sided.
- **Every believer has a gift; no believer has every gift** — this mirrors
  the exact resolution pattern used in the existing tongues paper (for
  1 Corinthians 12:30) and this run's prophecy paper (for the same verse
  applied to prophesying). I used the identical move here since it's the
  same passage doing the same rhetorical work, and it seemed more honest
  to reuse a resolution the corpus itself repeats than to invent a new one
  for variety's sake.

## No disagreement flagged for this topic

Same situation as five-fold ministry: I looked specifically for a genuine
split (permanent-possession framing of a gift vs. situational-manifestation
framing; pro- vs. anti-impartation stances) and did not find one held by
two different named teachers. What I found instead was one teacher (Prince)
holding a nuanced, both/and position himself — affirming impartation as
real while also warning against its careless use. That's balance within a
single voice, not a corpus-wide disagreement, so I did not create a
placeholder for it. If you read the "Laying On of Hands and Impartation"
section and want it to carry more caution or more confidence, that's an
editorial preference to apply directly — there isn't a second corpus voice
pulling the other way that would need a flagged placeholder instead.

## Thin-coverage spots, stated honestly

- **The individual "revelation gifts" (word of knowledge, word of wisdom,
  discerning of spirits) as their own distinct topics** — same situation
  as flagged in the prophecy paper's notes: covered, but folded into
  Prince's general gifts material rather than developed as their own
  subject anywhere in the corpus.
- **Modern "impartation" as a distinct ministry practice/movement**
  (services specifically built around transferring anointing) — the corpus
  material here is entirely Prince's more classical, exegetical treatment
  of the biblical practice. If Rhemata ever wants to address the modern
  ministry-practice version of "impartation" specifically (which carries
  its own real controversy in charismatic circles), this corpus doesn't
  currently have material that engages it directly.
