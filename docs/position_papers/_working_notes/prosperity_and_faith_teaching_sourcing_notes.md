## The flagged disagreement — read this carefully, it's more nuanced than "two camps"

I want to be precise here because the real shape of this disagreement is
easy to flatten into a caricature it doesn't actually match.

**Derek Prince is NOT a crude prosperity preacher**, and I want that on
record because his volume in this cluster could read that way at a glance.
His material explicitly and repeatedly REJECTS the "wealth proves
righteousness" formula by name — he discusses Job's friends preaching
exactly this and being rebuked by God for it, across three separate
propositions from "Job And His Accusing Friends." He redefines biblical
"prosperity" away from American-style material comfort toward "successfully
accomplishing one's assigned task," explicitly noting Jesus and the
apostles didn't prosper by American standards. He also has real, explicit
warnings against making wealth a god, trusting in it, or seeking it by
wrong means ("The Conditions"). At the same time, he DOES affirm that the
atonement includes real financial and material blessing (the "Jesus became
poor that we might become rich" material, Deuteronomy 28 blessings,
curse-reversal restoring "health and prosperity") and describes God
"prospering his people in a staggering way" as a current, ongoing reality.
So: nuanced, both/and, genuinely holds real tension within one teacher.

**Vlad Savchuk lands in a similar both/and place, independently.** He has
one document ("If You're Praying Like This, STOP! It's Witchcraft")
that explicitly and sharply warns against prioritizing material blessing
over spiritual growth — strong language, calling that priority inversion
"witchcraft." He also has a separate document ("Pitch a Tent, Build an
Altar, Dig a Well") that describes three escalating "levels of prosperity"
and actively encourages believers toward "becoming very prosperous" in
finances, family, and health. Same tension Prince holds, arrived at
independently.

**Zac Poonen and Leonard Ravenhill pull noticeably harder in one direction
and don't show the same both/and balance.** Poonen: "believers should not
hanker for the old covenant's promises of physical wealth and health"
(explicit — he frames material prosperity as an OLD covenant category
largely superseded, not just secondary), "primary focus... should be
seeking the kingdom of heaven, rather than earthly blessings and
prosperity." Ravenhill, even sharper: "gain and prosperity" being confused
with godliness is "a false notion... true Christianity is centered on
sacrifice, not success," and "the church has become married to prosperity,
personality, and popularity, rather than poverty, prisons, and
persecutions." Neither of them, in what I found, holds the explicit
both/and balance Prince and Savchuk both independently strike — their
material reads as more consistently skeptical of material-increase
expectations, not as a mirror-image "prosperity is always wrong" position,
but genuinely further along that spectrum than the other two.

**What I actually flagged in the draft:** not "some teachers are for
prosperity teaching, some are against it" — that would misrepresent Prince
and Savchuk, who explicitly reject the crude version themselves. What I
flagged is a real difference in DEGREE OF EXPECTATION: how much material
increase should a believer actually anticipate, ask for, or treat as
normal, versus how much this teaching should redirect a believer's
attention toward spiritual riches as the real point, with material things
held much more loosely and expected much less. That's a real, substantive
difference even between teachers who all reject the crude formula. Please
read the placeholder in the draft with this nuance in mind — it's more
precise than a simple two-camp framing would suggest, and I tried to word
it to reflect that rather than oversimplify it.

  ground across Prince, Savchuk, and even Poonen (his "True Riches and Wise
  Fathers" document affirms faithfulness with material possessions as a
  real, positive thing, just in service of "true riches"). I built this
  section only from claims none of the four voices contradict.
- **The unresolved tension** — see above.
- **Common Objections** — the "isn't this just a fundraising tactic"
  answer leans on Savchuk's own "witchcraft" framing (restated in my own
  words, not quoted) plus 1 Timothy 6:5, which is my own scriptural
  addition, not something I found discussed at length in the matched
  corpus material itself.
- **What This Is Not Saying** — per the task instructions, given more room
  here than in the other four papers. The health-wealth-guarantee
  rejection and the prosperity-as-proof-of-faith rejection are both
  directly corpus-sourced (Prince's Job material, explicitly). The
  manipulative-giving-appeals paragraph draws on Savchuk's "witchcraft"
  framing and 1 Timothy 6:5, but the specific examples I gave (targeting
  people who can't afford it, promising guaranteed returns) are my own
  reasonable extrapolation of the principle, not lifted from a specific
  corpus statement describing those exact practices — flagging that since
  it's slightly beyond what's directly attested.

## Thin-coverage spots, stated honestly

- **Tithing as its own controversy** — Savchuk's "Putting an End to the
  Tithing Debate" document is a real, dedicated defense of NT tithing (a
  genuinely contested question in the wider church — whether a literal 10%
  applies under the New Covenant at all), but it's the only sustained
  treatment of that specific question in this cluster. I mentioned tithing
  only briefly and didn't build a dedicated section around the tithing
  debate itself, since one document isn't enough to represent it as a
  settled or even a fairly-covered sub-topic.
- **"Seed faith" giving specifically** (the named practice of giving a
  specific amount expecting a specific multiplied return) — not
  represented anywhere I could find in this corpus by that name or
  description. I referenced the general shape of the concern in "What This
  Is Not Saying" without claiming it's a corpus-addressed topic.
- **Positive confession specifically as applied to finances** ("decree and
  declare" wealth into being) — not represented independently here; what
  exists is Prince's confession-and-appropriation material from the
  healing paper's research, applied there to sickness, not distinctly
  reused for money in this cluster.
