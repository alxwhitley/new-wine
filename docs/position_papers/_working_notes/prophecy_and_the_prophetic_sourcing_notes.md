## Section-by-section sourcing

- **What It Is / Purpose Is to Build Up** — scripture-anchored, broadly
  consistent across Prince, Kolenda, Mumford, Moses. Real consensus.
- **Available to the Whole Church, Not a Closed Office** — this exact
  12:29-vs-14:31 resolution is explicitly Kolenda's own argument (he states
  "every true believer... enabling them to hear God's voice and prophesy,"
  citing Numbers 11:29 directly) and is structurally identical to how the
  existing tongues paper resolves the parallel 12:30 objection — I built
  this section the same way, applied to prophecy, which felt like the
  honest and consistent move rather than inventing a new resolution.
- **It Comes Through People, and People Are Imperfect** — this is
  specifically Dennis Moses's material ("no prophecy is 100% pure due to
  human imperfection," his one document, "BREAKING OF YOUR SHELL"). It is
  the single best-developed statement of this exact point in the corpus,
  and I leaned on it structurally, though the wording in the draft is my
  own. Worth knowing this section's backbone is really one document from
  one otherwise-unrepresented teacher.
- **Testing/weighing prophecy** — well-attested across Kolenda, Mumford
  (his nine guidelines for judging prophecy), and Moses (submit to someone
  mature in the gift). Real, multi-voice agreement — this is one of the
  stronger-consensus sections in the whole paper.

## The flagged disagreement

Different in character from the healing paper's flag — this one is a
difference of emphasis and instinct rather than a flat contradiction.

**One thread (Bob Mumford's "Three Levels of Prophecy," and consistent with
Dennis Moses's material):** treats directive prophecy — a specific word
giving personal life direction — as legitimate and expected, provided it is
confirmed by other mature prophetic voices. Mumford gives nine explicit
guidelines for judging a word. This is a real, fairly systematic framework,
not an offhand comment.

**The other thread (Derek Prince, in "The Chief Shepherd and His
Undershepherds - Part 1"):** shares his own personal testimony of making
major life decisions by relying directly on the Lord rather than a human
shepherd, explicitly warning against the pride and control risk in leaning
on a human intermediary for major decisions.

**Why I flagged this instead of resolving it:** these aren't stated as
directly arguing with each other — they're each making a point in their
own context — but they pull in real, opposite directions on a live
practical question: how much weight should a prophetic word be allowed to
carry when someone is deciding something big? I did not go looking for
outside historical context to sharpen this further; I'm flagging only what
the corpus itself actually contains on both sides. If you're aware of
broader history behind either of these two teaching threads that should
inform how firmly this paper states its position, that's exactly the kind
of context this placeholder is asking you to bring — I don't have grounds
to bring it in myself from corpus content alone.

## Thin-coverage spots, stated honestly

- **Words of knowledge and words of wisdom as their own distinct topic** —
  folded into the general prophecy material in the draft because that's
  genuinely how the corpus treats them (almost entirely inside Derek
  Prince's general revelation-gifts teaching, not as their own developed
  subject). If Rhemata ever wants a dedicated treatment of the
  knowledge/wisdom distinction specifically, this corpus doesn't yet
  support one beyond what's already folded in here.
- **Old Testament predictive prophecy / eschatology** — Derek Prince has a
  large, separate body of material here (Israel, end-times fulfillment,
  "The Prophetic Word Made More Sure") that I deliberately did NOT pull
  into this paper. It's really a different topic — prophecy as fulfilled
  scriptural prediction about Israel and the end times, not prophecy as a
  living gift exercised in the church today — and blending the two would
  have diluted both. Flagging in case Alex wants that as a future,
  separate paper; it's a substantial, well-covered body of material on its
  own.
- **The office of a prophet specifically** (as distinct from the gift of
  prophesying) — mentioned via Ephesians 4:11 and Mumford's "ministry of
  the prophet" material, but I kept this light on purpose since it
  overlaps directly with the five-fold ministry paper later in this run —
  didn't want to duplicate that paper's actual territory here.
