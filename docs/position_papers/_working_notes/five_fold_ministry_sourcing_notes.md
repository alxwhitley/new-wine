# Sourcing Notes — The Five-Fold Ministry

For Alex's eyes only. Not part of the draft, never merged into it.

Method: read-only SELECT against `propositions`/`documents` via
`SUPABASE_DB_URL`. An initial broad pass on "apostle/evangelist/pastor/
teacher" was too noisy — "pastor" and "teacher" are common generic words
outside this specific topic — so the real analysis used a tighter pattern
anchored on "five-fold," Ephesians 4:11-13, "equip the saints," and
apostle/prophet/evangelist/pastor/teacher appearing together as a list. No
writes performed.

**This is essentially a one-teacher paper, and it should be read that way.**
Derek Prince has a genuine, deep, dedicated series on this exact topic —
"Mobile Ministries - Apostles," "Mobile Ministries - Evangelists and
Teachers," "Mobile Ministries - Prophets," "Prophets, Evangelists,
Teachers," "Apostolic Teams," "Presbyteries and Apostolic Teams" (two
parts), "The Reproductive Cycle - Apostles and Elders," "Resident
Ministries - Shepherds and Deacons," "The Chief Shepherd and His
Undershepherds" (two parts), "The New Testament Evangelist," "Appoint
Elders In Every City," "The Maturing and Completion of Christ's Body" — at
least a dozen documents built specifically around this framework. Nobody
else in the corpus develops it independently at anything close to this
depth. Kolenda, Mumford, Kreighbaum, and Savchuk each contribute a
proposition or two that's consistent with Prince's framework, but none of
them build out their own independent treatment of it.

**Read the whole draft as Derek Prince's systematic teaching on this topic,
restated in Rhemata's voice** — not as an average of multiple independent
teachers. That's different from every other paper in this run, where at
least a second substantial voice existed. Here there effectively isn't one.

## Section-by-section sourcing

- **What It Is / the five distinct definitions** — Prince, almost entirely.
  His "Mobile Ministries" series defines each of the five individually and
  in unusual depth (word origins, frequency counts in the Greek text,
  etc.) — I compressed and restated this in my own words, but the
  underlying definitions and distinctions are his.
- **Mobile vs. resident ministries** — this specific framing (apostle/
  prophet/evangelist/teacher as itinerant "mobile" ministries vs. shepherd
  as "resident") is Prince's own structural device, not a term I found
  anywhere else in the corpus or a standard term I recognize from outside
  it either. Worth knowing this is a fairly distinctive way of organizing
  the five, specific to this one teacher's framework, presented in the
  draft as settled teaching without attribution per this session's no-names
  rule.
- **Authority through service, not control (plural eldership, apostles not
  dictating, 1 Peter 5:3)** — also Prince-sourced (his "Reproductive Cycle,"
  "Appoint Elders In Every City," "Chief Shepherd" material), reinforced
  rather than contradicted by one Vlad Savchuk proposition warning against
  manipulative leaders using scripture to control people. Consistent, not
  independently corroborated in depth.

## No disagreement flagged for this topic

Unlike the other four papers in this run, I did not find a genuine
two-sided split in the corpus on this topic, and I did not manufacture one
to match the pattern of the other papers. What I found instead was
consistent, single-voice-dominant material with no real corpus pushback
anywhere.

Derek Prince has one in-corpus proposition ("A Time Of Restoration")
claiming the five ministry gifts are being historically "restored" to the
church in a specific order — teachers and pastors first, evangelists next,
apostles and prophets last. This is a specific historical/theological claim
(associated with a broader "restorationist" strand of charismatic teaching)
that implies these offices were largely absent from church history for a
long stretch before being progressively recovered. That sits in some real
tension with the strongly continuationist framing used elsewhere across
this corpus (e.g. the existing baptism-of-the-Spirit paper's own
cessationism section, and Daniel Kolenda's material throughout this run),
which argues these gifts never actually ceased at all — just went
unpracticed by traditions that stopped believing in them. "Restored after a
real absence" and "never ceased, just neglected" aren't quite the same
claim. Originally this was backed by exactly one thin proposition, so
rather than build it into a section or an editorial placeholder on that
thin footing, it was left out of the draft entirely.

**Updated:** additional independent material, reviewed and approved outside
the existing database corpus, affirms the same restoration framing
directly and at length — not a passing aside but a developed argument for
a genuine historical gap before these offices returned. That's enough to
promote this from a deliberately-omitted aside to a real, flagged
disagreement. It's now stated as an explicit editorial placeholder in the
draft itself (see the "An unresolved question worth naming honestly"
section) rather than left out.

## New source material used in this round of edits

Additional teaching content, reviewed and approved outside the existing
database corpus, contributed the material behind the "gift vs. calling is
issued, not earned" framing, the "outgrown, not performed" purpose
paragraph, and the stated position on the limits of modern apostolic
authority (that a modern apostle's authority is real but not equivalent to
the Scripture-writing authority of the original apostles). None of that
came from a fresh database query this session — flagging clearly so it
isn't mistaken for corpus-sourced material if this note is revisited later.

## Thin-coverage spots, stated honestly

- **The whole topic**, frankly — see above. This paper is thinner than any
  other in this run. It reads well because Prince's own material on it is
  unusually organized and complete, not because the corpus broadly
  supports it.
- **Modern application / how a local church should actually implement
  five-fold leadership day to day** — the corpus material is mostly
  definitional and biblical-exegetical (what each calling is, where it's
  found in scripture) rather than practical (how a real congregation
  structures itself around this). The draft stays at the same level of
  altitude as the source material for this reason.
